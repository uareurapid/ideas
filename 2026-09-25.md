## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: ReturnTrack**
Online shoppers miss the 30-day return window, lose the "return by" date, and forget whether the refund ever landed. ReturnTrack logs every online order with its return deadline, generates return labels in one tap, and tracks the refund from "dropped off" to "money back in your account."

**Idea 2: InsureProof**
When disaster strikes, nobody can remember everything they owned, and insurance claims get denied or underpaid for lack of proof. InsureProof is a room-by-room photo inventory of your possessions with serial numbers, receipts, and estimated values, so you can file a complete claim in minutes instead of months.

**Idea 3: PotluckPlan**
Every potluck has three people bringing coleslaw and nobody bringing a main dish. PotluckPlan lets hosts post what's needed and guests claim dishes, with automatic duplicate warnings and dietary-tag filters, so the party actually has a balanced spread.

**Idea 4: WarrantyWise**
People throw away receipts and can't remember if a broken appliance is still covered, and recalls go unnoticed. WarrantyWise stores receipts and purchase dates per product, tracks warranty windows, and alerts you to recalls and safety notices before something breaks — or worse, hurts someone.

**Idea 5: KinTech**
Millions of adult children are the unpaid IT department for aging parents, but they can't see the screen when something goes wrong. KinTech is a guided remote-support tool that lets you walk a parent through fixes with big-button on-screen prompts, screenshot sharing, and a shared "tech notes" log of every password and quirk.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: TestimonialForge 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Indie founders know reviews and testimonials drive signups, but asking for them feels awkward and the results sit scattered across emails, DMs, and app-store comments. TestimonialForge detects happy moments (upgrades, milestone usage, support praise), auto-requests a review at the right time, and turns raw customer messages into polished, permissioned testimonials for your landing page, G2, Capterra, and Product Hunt.

**Idea 7: ChurnGuard 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Most churned customers never tell you why — they just cancel and vanish, so founders fix the wrong thing. ChurnGuard runs a frictionless offboarding survey at the moment of cancellation, buckets the reasons, and automatically triggers win-back offers and personalized follow-ups for the "price too high" or "missing feature" cohorts before they're gone for good.

**Idea 8: PriceLab 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Indie devs price their product by guessing, leaving money on the table or scaring customers away. PriceLab lets you A/B test pricing tiers and trial lengths live, run willingness-to-pay surveys, and see revenue-per-cohort so you stop guessing and start pricing off data instead of vibes.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: CQLog 📱 MOBILE APP**
Amateur radio ("ham") operators still log contacts on paper or in clunky 1990s desktop software, and chasing awards like "Worked All States" or DXCC is a manual spreadsheet slog. CQLog is a modern mobile QSO logger that auto-fills band, mode, and signal reports, tracks your progress toward awards with gamified badges, and logs the propagation conditions so you know when that rare station is actually reachable. Niche: millions of licensed hams worldwide with almost no polished mobile tools — existing logging apps are dated and joyless, and the community's "73" sign-offs, antenna/HOA feuds, and ragchew culture are primed for in-app jokes and shareable "finally worked Japan!" flexes.

**Idea 10: DiceGoblin 📱 MOBILE APP**
Every TTRPG player has hoarded more dice sets than they'll ever need and secretly believes one d20 is cursed. DiceGoblin is a dice collection tracker (brand, set, material, glitter level) plus a rolling-stats journal that logs your d20 rolls so you can prove — scientifically — that your dice are the reason you failed three saves in a row, complete with "cursed" and "lucky" designations and a retirement ceremony for dice that betray you. Niche: the massive, obsessive D&D/dice-collector subculture has no dice-specific app, competition is effectively zero, and "my dice hate me, here's 200 rolls of data" is inherently screenshot-worthy.
