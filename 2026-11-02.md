## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: AllergenMatrix**
Cafes and small restaurants are legally required to know the 14 allergens in every dish, but the matrix lives in a laminated folder that goes stale the moment a supplier swaps a sauce. AllergenMatrix maps ingredients to allergens, ingests supplier spec sheets, flags any dish with missing data, and regenerates the counter folder plus a QR menu after every menu change. It turns a fine-waiting-to-happen into a five-minute weekly check.

**Idea 2: PhotoDay**
School photographers still run picture day on paper order forms, ten-character access codes, and a spreadsheet of sibling groups, so parents lose codes and photographers chase retakes for weeks. PhotoDay gives the photographer a shoot-day roster with class order, sibling linking, and consent flags, then sends each parent one link with proofs, orders, and retake booking. Schools get a single status page instead of forty emails asking where the photos are.

**Idea 3: StreetPermit**
Putting a skip, scaffold, container, or crane on a public road means a different council form, fee, and parking suspension process in every borough, and homeowners find out too late that the delivery lorry cannot legally stop. StreetPermit asks for the postcode and the object, returns the exact permits, lead times, and costs, pre-fills the application pack, and tracks expiry so the skip does not become an offence. One place for the most annoying part of every renovation.

**Idea 4: DischargeDay**
Hospital discharges happen fast, and families go home with a folder of appointments, a hired commode arriving "sometime", and no shared idea of who is doing what. DischargeDay builds the discharge checklist with the ward nurse, splits tasks across family members, tracks equipment delivery and follow-up bookings, and keeps a single number for the district nurse to call. The gap between leaving the ward and being safe at home is where readmissions live.

**Idea 5: SafeCheck**
Grassroots sports clubs and youth groups have to prove every coach has a valid DBS/PVG check, safeguarding training, and first-aid certificate, but the evidence sits in a treasurer's spreadsheet until an audit or an incident. SafeCheck stores each volunteer's checks and expiry dates, chases renewals automatically, and produces the evidence pack a league or insurer asks for. It protects the kids, and it protects the volunteer who forgot to renew.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: CSVGate 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Every SaaS has an importer that works on the founder's clean export and dies on a customer's file with a BOM, semicolon delimiters, merged cells, `dd/mm` dates, and a formula that starts with `=`. CSVGate is a CI harness that fuzzes your importer with a corpus of real-world messy spreadsheets, asserts nothing silently drops or misparses, and reports the exact row and encoding that broke. It turns "the import is broken" support tickets into a failing test before the customer ever uploads.

**Idea 7: InviteLint 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Calendar invites look correct in code and then duplicate, vanish, or land an hour off in Outlook, Google Calendar, and Apple Calendar, especially around recurring events, timezone changes, and cancellations. InviteLint sends your generated `.ics` files through real clients, diffs what each one actually created, and flags invalid RRULEs, missing UIDs, and broken cancellation sequences. Because "the meeting is at the wrong time" is the least debuggable bug in software.

**Idea 8: RecallGuard 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Switching embedding models or chunking strategy silently changes retrieval quality, and teams discover it weeks later from angry users rather than a test. RecallGuard replays a golden query set against the old and new index, measures recall, ranking drift, and missing documents, and estimates re-embedding cost before you commit. It makes an embedding migration a measurable deploy instead of a leap of faith.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: BenchMark 📱 MOBILE APP**
Niche: memorial bench spotters — people who read the little plaques on park benches, photograph them, and wonder who the person was. BenchMark logs each bench with GPS, plaque photo, inscription, and view rating, builds a personal map, and lets people leave a quiet note when a bench belongs to someone they knew. Low competition because the audience is small and organised entirely through scattered Flickr albums and local history blogs. Funny/viral because plaques range from devastating to deranged ("for Derek, who hated this view"), and "best bench in the country" rankings are irresistible sharebait.

**Idea 10: MothNight 📱 MOBILE APP**
Niche: moth recorders and light-trap keepers — a stubbornly dedicated community that still submits counts to county recorders in spreadsheets and emailed photos. MothNight logs a trap session, suggests species from a photo, counts individuals, records weather and moon phase, and exports a recorder-ready file. Low competition because the hobby is nocturnal, hyper-local, and served by paper forms and a decade-old website. Funny/viral because trap results are absurd ("eleven species and one very confused wasp"), the ID arguments are vicious, and a "moth of the night" feed is weirdly beautiful.
