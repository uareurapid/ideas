## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: ParcelSafe**
Neighborhood package protection network. When you're not home, verified neighbors accept and hold your deliveries. Built-in photo proof, karma points, and optional tipping. No hardware lockers needed — just trust plus accountability.

**Idea 2: HarvestShare**
Home gardeners drown in zucchini every August while neighbors buy the same veggies at the store. A hyperlocal produce exchange where people list surplus homegrown food, others claim it for free or small cash. Think Olio but exclusively for backyard harvests. Reduces food waste and builds actual community.

**Idea 3: ObituaryAI**
Writing an obituary or eulogy while grieving is brutal. Family members answer guided questions about the deceased, and the tool drafts a dignified, personalized tribute. Pulls in public records for factual accuracy. Outputs ready-to-publish obituaries, funeral program text, and eulogy drafts. Hard moment, made easier.

**Idea 4: SlowHour**
Real-time "empty tables" deals from local cafes and restaurants. Businesses flag when foot traffic dies and offer time-limited discounts (e.g., "30% off coffee, next 45 minutes"). Users get push notifications when their favorite spots have slow hours. Restaurants fill empty seats, customers get deals. Win-win that DoorDash can't replicate.

**Idea 5: ReloReferral**
When someone moves cities, they lose their network of trusted providers — dentist, mechanic, barber, accountant. This lets people curate and "gift" their go-to provider list to newcomers, with personal notes like "Maria cuts curly hair and doesn't make small talk." A referral network for the boring-but-essential services that actually determine quality of life in a new city.
