## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: PlotQueue**
Allotment waiting lists run 3–10 years and applicants have zero visibility — councils don't publish positions, and people forget which sites they applied to. PlotQueue tracks applications across every local site, shows typical wait times from crowd data, and alerts when a plot vacancy opens or your position jumps. It also flags sites with short lists that most gardeners overlook.

**Idea 2: Offcut**
Tradespeople and DIYers throw away usable leftover bricks, timber, tiles, and insulation because selling small quantities isn't worth the effort. Offcut is a hyperlocal marketplace for surplus building materials measured in "enough to finish a small job," with photo upload, quantity tags, and same-week pickup. Builders offload waste, renovators get cheap materials, and skips get lighter.

**Idea 3: CostumeCall**
Dance and theatre studios chase parents for recital costume sizes, deposits, and late orders across WhatsApp, paper forms, and bank transfers — then guess quantities and eat the cost of extras. CostumeCall collects sizes and payments in one link, builds the batch order per costume supplier, and tracks delivery and fit swaps before show day. Small studios get professional ops without buying a full studio-management suite.

**Idea 4: SkipSplit**
Renting a skip for one small clear-out is expensive, and neighbours usually have their own mini clear-outs at the same time. SkipSplit lets nearby households pool one skip: split the cost, book a shared permit window, and track who has filled what share. It also matches people with the same waste type so nobody pays for a half-empty container.

**Idea 5: SurveySpeak**
Homebuyers get a 60-page survey full of hedge-trimming caveats and "further investigation advised," with no sense of what actually matters or what it will cost. SurveySpeak turns the report into a prioritized fix list with rough cost ranges, flags the three things to renegotiate on, and drafts the questions to send back to the surveyor. It's a decoder, not another generic moving checklist.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: DocGap 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Every docs site has a search box that quietly records what users wanted and didn't find, but nobody mines it. DocGap ingests search logs, clusters zero-result and low-click queries, and turns them into a ranked documentation backlog with suggested page outlines — plus a weekly "top unanswered question" alert. It turns support pain into docs work with evidence instead of guesswork.

**Idea 7: EventDrift 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Analytics events silently rot: someone renames `signup_completed`, drops a property, or ships an event with an email address in the payload, and dashboards lie for months. EventDrift compares your tracking plan against the code and runtime payloads in CI, comments on the PR with added/removed/renamed events and PII risks, and blocks merges that break key funnels. Product and data teams stop discovering breakage a quarter later.

**Idea 8: EraseProof 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
When a customer exercises GDPR/CCPA deletion, SaaS teams manually delete rows across Postgres, S3, logs, warehouses, and a dozen vendors — then have no clean proof it happened. EraseProof runs deletion workflows across connected systems, collects per-system confirmations, retries stragglers, and generates an auditor-ready deletion receipt with timestamps. It turns a quarterly compliance fire drill into a repeatable, provable job.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: Antenna 📱 MOBILE APP**
Ant keepers track queens, brood stages, colony counts, feeding, temperature, and diapause in notes and spreadsheets, then lose the thread when a colony stalls or a queen dies. Antenna is a colony log built around the actual hobby: per-species care presets, molt and brood timelines, a "colony health" score, and a captive-bred trade board for queens and colonies. Niche: the fast-growing ant-keeping and invert community (huge YouTube scene, almost no dedicated app); low competition because generic pet apps don't model colonies; funny and viral because time-lapse growth, dramatic queen deaths, and species flexing are made for sharing.

**Idea 10: StiffCompetition 📱 MOBILE APP**
Amateur taxidermists juggle specimen intake, freezer space, tanning and maceration stages, drying times, and show deadlines in their heads — and disaster smells follow. StiffCompetition logs each specimen through every stage with timers and reminders, tracks freezer capacity, stores supplier and CITES/legal notes, and builds a portfolio for show entries. Niche: taxidermy hobbyists and small workshops; essentially zero competition on the App Store; inherently funny because a well-timed "your specimen is ready to mount" notification is peak dark humour for a community that already jokes about it.
