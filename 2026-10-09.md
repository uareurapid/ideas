## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: AfterFile**
When someone dies, the family is left scrambling to find passwords, account lists, the will, insurance papers, and funeral wishes — usually at the worst possible moment. AfterFile is a private "digital executor" binder: you log accounts, assets, documents, and end-of-life wishes once, and it releases a guided, organized handover to the person you designate when the time comes. Turns a painful scavenger hunt into a clear roadmap nobody wants to need but everyone should have.

**Idea 2: ShelfSync**
Library power-users juggle multiple cards across county, school, and city systems, each with different due dates, holds, and renewal rules — and quietly rack up fines. ShelfSync links all your library cards, shows every due date and hold in one place, auto-renews what can be renewed, and pings you when a hold is ready to pick up. The "everything bookmarked, nothing tabbed" cure for the public-library crowd.

**Idea 3: LendShed**
Every neighborhood has $10,000 worth of tools, ladders, and power washers sitting idle in garages, while a neighbor down the street buys the same thing for one afternoon. LendShed is a hyperlocal borrow/lend board for tools and equipment, with condition photos, deposits, and a repair/return log so "you break it, you bought it" never turns into a feud. Turns every block into a free tool library.

**Idea 4: VetVault**
Pet owners can't find a vaccine record when boarding, a med schedule when traveling, or a microchip number when a pet goes missing — it's scattered across vet portals, paper files, and memory. VetVault is a single record for each pet: vaccines, medications with reminders, microchip and insurance details, and shareable vet-visit summaries. One tidy place for the "where's my cat's shot record?" scramble every owner knows.

**Idea 5: HiveMind**
Beekeepers track hive inspections, queen status, mite counts, and swarm season across scattered notebooks and memory, and a single missed check can collapse a colony. HiveMind logs each inspection with queen-spotting photos, tracks pest and disease treatment schedules, and predicts swarm risk from weather and brood patterns. A genuinely underserved, deeply passionate niche currently living in spreadsheets.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: OnboardPulse 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
SaaS founders see signups pour in but have no idea where new users stall before their "aha" moment — the funnel goes dark right after the signup page. OnboardPulse instruments your onboarding flow to show drop-off per step, time-to-activation per cohort, and which setup roadblocks quietly kill retention. Turns "why does nobody activate?" into a ranked fix list instead of a guess.

**Idea 7: A11ySweep 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Accessibility is now legally mandatory for many products (EU Accessibility Act, ADA lawsuits), but most teams have no idea if their UI actually passes until a complaint or fine lands. A11ySweep runs WCAG audits as a CI check on every PR, flags contrast, focus-order, and ARIA failures, and suggests fixes before they ship. Compliance as a pipeline gate, not an afterthought.

**Idea 8: CrowdRadar 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Founders know their ideal customer is *somewhere* online but waste weeks guessing which subreddits, Discords, Slacks, and newsletters they actually read and complain in. CrowdRadar searches communities by keyword, surfaces where your ICP is active and what specific gripes they keep repeating, and turns those complaints into messaging and lead lists. "Find your people and their pain" before you spend a dollar on ads.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: StarterPet 📱 MOBILE APP**
A sourdough starter is basically a pet you keep in a jar — it needs regular feeding, warmth, and a name — and millions of bakers kill theirs by forgetting it in the fridge. StarterPet tracks feedings, schedules discards, logs temperature and rise, and gives your starter a name and a mood ("Brad is hungry. Brad will die. Again.") with an obituary when you neglect it. Niche: the massive sourdough-baking community has no dedicated app; low competition because recipe apps ignore starter upkeep entirely, and the "I killed another starter" guilt is inherently meme-worthy.

**Idea 10: WildScran 📱 MOBILE APP**
Foragers want to know what's edible right now, where it grows, and — critically — whether that mushroom is dinner or a trip to the ER. WildScran maps seasonal forage spots, logs finds with GPS and photos, runs community ID checks, and gives every find a "scran or scram" verdict with a don't-die warning and local legality notes. Niche: urban foraging is booming but has almost no polished app; low competition because the big players skip it, and "scran or scram" is a built-in viral catchphrase for an audience that lives to post their haul.
