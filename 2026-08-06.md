## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: ScamCallBingo**
Scam calls are universal and infuriating — so gamify them. Printable or on-screen bingo cards featuring common scam script phrases ("your social security number," "IRS," "refund department," "kindly"). The community submits and votes on card templates for different scam archetypes (tech support, delivery, customs, romance). Turns powerless frustration into collective entertainment.

**Idea 2: ShelfMap**
IKEA and other big-box stores are intentionally disorienting labyrinths. A community-mapped navigation tool where shoppers document and rate hidden shortcuts — the "skip the showroom, straight to the warehouse" routes, the secret back passages, the fastest path from entrance to checkout for specific items. Think Waze for giant retail spaces. User-mapped routes are fair game legally, and no good competitor exists.

**Idea 3: MoveRecall**
Moving means disconnecting and reconnecting dozens of devices, cables, and furniture assemblies. An app that guides you through photographing every connection point, cable routing, and assembly state before you disassemble anything — so when you arrive exhausted in the new place, you can reference exactly how everything was set up. Like a specialized reverse-engineering journal for your own stuff, saving hours of confusion.

**Idea 4: WillDispute**
Nobody talks about unfair wills until it's too late, and inheritance battles tear families apart. A guided platform for families to document informal discussions about estate division before death — with templates, conflict-resolution prompts, and mediator directories. Addresses the massive, uncomfortable gap between legal wills and actual family expectations. The inheritance dispute market is huge, emotional, and completely underserved by tech.

**Idea 5: PollenMap**
Allergy sufferers choose neighborhoods, apartments, parks, and even vacation spots based on pollen, but all they get are vague city-wide forecasts. A hyperlocal pollen map combining crowdsourced symptom reports, wind data, vegetation maps, and satellite imagery to show actual "sneeze zones" at street-level resolution. Massively more useful than generic pollen counts and no one is doing it at this granularity.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: DemoClean 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Every developer has accidentally leaked API keys, passwords, or customer PII in a recorded demo or livestream. A tool that pre-processes screen recordings before sharing — auto-blurring sensitive fields, .env values, terminal output, email addresses, browser autofill, bookmark bars, and notification popups. Solves the universal "oh god did I just show my Stripe secret key on Twitch" panic that every dev experiences at least once.

**Idea 7: LicenseRot 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Open source projects accumulate dependencies whose licenses silently change — an MIT package goes BUSL, a permissive lib adds a restrictive clause in v3. A CI/CD tool that audits your entire dependency tree for license compliance changes, diffs license text between versions, catches copyleft contamination risks, and generates compliance reports. Most devs ship products without knowing a critical dependency changed its license 3 versions ago.

**Idea 8: HatTip 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Open source maintainers do incredible work but get zero credit in the products built on their libraries. A tool that auto-generates a beautifully designed "Built With" credits page for any SaaS product — scanning package.json/requirements.txt, explaining what each dependency does, linking to sponsorship pages and contributor profiles. Developers get credit, users see transparency, and you build goodwill in the OSS community. Think "npm thanks" meets About page generator.
