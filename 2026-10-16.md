## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: RiderLog**
Motorcyclists track service by mileage, not calendar time, and most owners' manuals, tire logs, and track-day notes live in a paper booklet that gets lost. RiderLog is a bike-specific maintenance log: it predicts the next service window from your actual odometer, tracks tire wear and chain lube intervals, and keeps a photo history you can hand to a buyer to prove the bike was cared for.

**Idea 2: DarkSkyAlert**
Stargazers and astrophotographers have to cross-reference three apps (cloud cover, moon phase, light pollution, seeing) just to answer "is tonight worth driving 40 minutes out of the city?" DarkSkyAlert watches your saved dark-sky spots and pings you only when a genuinely good window is coming — clear skies, no moon, and a "go now" score — so you stop missing the handful of good nights a year.

**Idea 3: TagDraw**
Hunters must enter state tag/license draws (elk, antelope, turkey) that open and close on wildly different dates across dozens of jurisdictions, and missing one means waiting a whole year. TagDraw tracks every draw deadline for the states you hunt, remembers your preference points, and nudges you before applications close with the fee and required documents.

**Idea 4: PensionTrace**
Job-hoppers routinely lose track of old workplace pensions and 401(k)s, and the money sits dormant for decades. PensionTrace walks you through locating orphaned accounts across former employers and jurisdictions, generates the letter/forms each provider needs, and tracks the transfer so you actually consolidate instead of just wondering where it went.

**Idea 5: ShiftBody**
Nurses, factory workers, and pilots on rotating shifts fight a circadian rhythm that never resets, and generic sleep advice doesn't fit a 3am start. ShiftBody builds a per-rota plan: when to sleep, when to get light, when to cut caffeine, and when to eat, adjusting automatically each time your shift pattern rotates.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: CostDiff 🛠️**
Nobody notices a cloud bill change until the invoice lands weeks later, because infra PRs and new dependencies are reviewed for correctness, not cost. CostDiff runs on every pull request, estimates the monthly cloud-cost delta (new instances, storage classes, egress, API calls), and comments "this PR adds ~$340/mo" with the line items — so finance surprises become a merge-time conversation.

**Idea 7: FlagJanitor 🛠️**
Feature flags accumulate forever: fully-rolled-out flags, unreferenced flags, and dead code branches behind them, all quietly rotting in the codebase. FlagJanitor scans the repo (and your flag provider) for stale flags, proves they're at 100%/0% rollout, and opens a cleanup PR that removes the flag and the dead branch, with a report of what it safely deleted.

**Idea 8: DocDrift 🛠️**
Docs go stale silently — an API signature changes, a config key is renamed, a screenshot is years old, and the README confidently lies to every new user. DocDrift diffs code changes against the docs that reference them, flags the specific sections that no longer match, and opens a documentation PR with the corrected snippet so docs stay trustworthy without a human re-reading everything.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: TowerPull 📱 MOBILE APP**
Niche: change-ringers (bell ringers) — roughly 40,000 people worldwide who ring church tower bells, a community with essentially no purpose-built mobile tooling and a heavily "paper and chalkboard" culture. TowerPull finds nearby towers and practice nights, teaches and drills ringing "methods" with a phone simulation, and logs quarter-peals for the Ringing World records. Low competition because the audience is tiny and hyper-specialized — existing bells-and-ropes software is desktop-era or nonexistent. Funny/viral because bell ringing is deeply weird to outsiders: it's a team sport played on medieval instruments, with absurd jargon ("hurry hard" is curling but "the tenor's gone", "bob single", "peal board"), so the "explain your hobby to a normal person" content writes itself.

**Idea 10: HurryHard 📱 MOBILE APP**
Niche: amateur curling clubs and bonspiel (tournament) players — a fast-growing but digitally neglected sport whose members still coordinate leagues over email threads and paper draw sheets. HurryHard lets clubs schedule leagues and draws, find nearby bonspiels to enter, and track team standings and individual stats (hammer, steals, ends won). Low competition because curling is regional and the community is small enough that no serious app has bothered, while clubs desperately need a better than-spreadsheet option. Funny/viral because curling slang is genuinely ridiculous and endlessly quotable ("hurry hard!", "the hammer", "bonspiel", "hack weight"), making it ideal for shareable "our sport has the best words" content.
