## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: LostBin**
Schools, gyms, pools, and office buildings accumulate mountains of lost property and manage it with a shelf and a Facebook post, so staff field the same "did anyone hand in a black hoodie?" calls all week. LostBin lets staff photograph and tag items at intake, gives owners a searchable "is my thing here?" page, and auto-generates donation or disposal notices once the holding period ends. It saves staff hours and stops decent jackets going in the bin.

**Idea 2: ShortageScan**
People on ADHD medication, HRT, or GLP-1s call a dozen pharmacies and still come home empty-handed during shortages. ShortageScan lets patients share a prescription and see nearby pharmacies reporting stock or a fresh "got it here today" signal, with a one-tap hold request. Pharmacies get one demand signal instead of forty identical phone calls.

**Idea 3: LeagueNight**
Volunteer league secretaries for bowling, darts, and pool run standings, handicaps, fixtures, and subs in a spreadsheet that breaks every season. LeagueNight is phone-first league admin: teams enter scores at the alley, handicaps and standings update themselves, and fixtures reschedule around venue availability. Built for the one person doing it for free who threatens to quit every year.

**Idea 4: DonateRight**
Charities reject half of what people haul to the door, and good stuff gets dumped because nobody knows who takes a broken microwave or an old piano. DonateRight checks an item, its condition, and your postcode against local charity, reuse, and repair options, books pickups, and says honestly when the answer is landfill. Less wasted Saturday, fewer sagging bags outside charity shops.

**Idea 5: UniformSwap**
School uniforms are absurdly expensive and barely worn before kids outgrow them, but second-hand exchange dies in chaotic parent Facebook groups because nobody can match crest, colour, and size. UniformSwap is a school-scoped marketplace where parents list by item, size, and logo, get matched with nearby families, and arrange handover at the gate. Schools can run a sponsored no-cost rail for families who need it.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: DarkPreview 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Transactional emails look fine in the designer's browser and turn into black-on-black logos and invisible buttons in Gmail and Outlook dark mode. DarkPreview renders every template across dark-mode clients (forced inversion, partial inversion, image blocking), flags the exact broken elements, and shows the CSS fix. It runs in CI so the password-reset email never ships unreadable again.

**Idea 7: LinkProof 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Universal links and Android app links break silently: a changed bundle ID, a missing association file, or one extra redirect turns every marketing link into a blank Safari page. LinkProof crawls your link inventory on real iOS and Android devices, verifies association files, redirects, and fallback behaviour, and alerts before a campaign sends people to the wrong place. One dashboard answers "does this link open the app or the website?"

**Idea 8: ModelFit 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Mobile developers want on-device AI but cannot tell whether a 4B model will run at usable speed on a mid-range Android phone until they have built the whole thing. ModelFit benchmarks candidate models and quantizations against a device profile (RAM, NPU, thermal throttling) and returns tokens per second, memory headroom, and battery cost before you commit. It stops "great on the newest iPhone, unusable on a Pixel 7a" from being a launch-week discovery.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: Mudlark 📱 MOBILE APP**
Niche: foreshore and riverbank scavengers — mudlarks, beachcombers, and bottle diggers who hunt clay pipes, coins, and mystery metal in the mud. Mudlark logs finds with GPS, tide window, and photos, auto-generates the museum or Portable Antiquities Scheme report, and alerts you to low tides at your saved spots. Low competition because the hobby is hyper-local, permit-gated, and currently runs on paper, forum threads, and group chats. Funny/viral because "rate my mud" and a feed of genuinely baffling objects ("coin? button? ancient curse?") is native sharebait.

**Idea 10: OnOn 📱 MOBILE APP**
Niche: Hash House Harriers, the "drinking club with a running problem" — thousands of chapters worldwide still coordinating hares, trails, and hash cash through email chains and a decades-old website. OnOn handles hare rotation, trail announcements, attendance, and the down-down fines ledger, plus a first-timer explainer so newbies are not completely lost. Low competition because the culture is proudly chaotic and nobody has built serious tooling for it. Funny/viral because naming ceremonies, arbitrary fines, and ritualized arguing are endlessly clippable — "I got fined for wearing the wrong socks" is a whole genre.
