## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: RentShield**
Rental car companies routinely blame renters for pre-existing dings, and nobody walks the car with a flashlight before driving off. RentShield is a guided 2-minute photo/video walk-around that time-and-GPS-stamps every scratch, then generates a ready-to-send dispute PDF if a damage claim arrives weeks later. A boring, recurring anxiety solved with one habit.

**Idea 2: DeadSpot**
Everyone has that one room where the Wi-Fi dies, but nobody knows why or where to put a mesh node. DeadSpot turns a slow lap around the house into a live heat-map of signal strength, flags dead zones, and recommends exact router/mesh placement. Cheap utility for the millions who just live with buffering.

**Idea 3: PermitLog**
Teenagers need 40–60 supervised driving hours for a learner's permit, and parents currently track it on a scrap of paper — or fabricate it the night before the test. PermitLog auto-logs each drive with GPS, time, and road conditions, tags required skills (night driving, highway, parking), and exports the state-ready log. Solves a real, universal, unglamorous chore.

**Idea 4: ChapterMeet**
Book clubs die because nobody can pick a date, vote on a book, or remember who's hosting. ChapterMeet handles scheduling polls, book nomination with ranked voting, discussion prompts, and attendance — so the club spends time on the book instead of the logistics. Small but loyal niche with almost no dedicated software.

**Idea 5: PedalVault**
Bikes get stolen and rarely recovered because there's no easy registry of serial numbers and photos tied to the owner. PedalVault registers your bike's serial, frame number, and photos, then monitors resale marketplaces for matching listings and alerts you plus local groups. A community-safety tool with a clear emotional hook.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: StandupSync 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Async and remote teams still waste 15 minutes a day on standup that rehashes what anyone could read from the repo. StandupSync pulls each dev's commits, merged PRs, and ticket activity and auto-generates a concise "yesterday/today/blockers" summary for the channel — or feeds it straight into a Slack/Linear update. Kills the meeting, keeps the visibility.

**Idea 7: DeadCodeSweep 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Every codebase accumulates orphaned functions, unused exports, and imports nobody dares delete because "someone might need it." DeadCodeSweep statically analyzes the repo, scores each symbol's "deadness" confidence, and opens a safe removal PR with a rollback path. Shrinks the codebase and quiets the "what is this even for?" arguments.

**Idea 8: LeakScan 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Developers accidentally commit API keys, tokens, and cloud credentials to git history every day — and deleting the commit doesn't remove it from history. LeakScan scans the full git history (not just the working tree) for leaked secrets, identifies which are live and need rotation, and generates a remediation checklist. Post-breach triage every team should run but almost never does.
