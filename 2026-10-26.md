## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: Renamed**
Changing your name after marriage, divorce, or a deed poll means updating banks, passports, insurers, payroll, subscriptions, and loyalty accounts — each with different proof requirements — and most people discover the stragglers months later when something fails. Renamed builds a country-aware checklist, generates the exact letters and forms each institution asks for, and tracks what is done, pending, and rejected. It can also keep watching for old-name mail and flag accounts you forgot.

**Idea 2: WheelTime**
Learner drivers and their parents must log dozens of supervised hours across night, motorway, and bad-weather conditions, but paper logs are vague and instructors cannot see what has actually been practised. WheelTime logs sessions by road type and manoeuvre, shows a test-readiness heatmap, and suggests the next practice route to fill the gaps. It exports a clean summary for the instructor or test centre.

**Idea 3: PotholeProof**
Hitting a pothole can cost hundreds in tyres and alignment, but council claim forms are buried, deadlines are short, and most people give up. PotholeProof captures timestamped photos, GPS, damage quotes, and the correct road authority, then assembles a claim pack and follows up until it is paid or refused. It also maps the pothole so neighbours can add evidence.

**Idea 4: HenKit**
Organising a hen or bachelorette weekend in a group chat means lost deposits, dietary chaos, and one person quietly covering everything. HenKit collects RSVPs, budgets, sizes and preferences, and payments in one place, then builds a shared itinerary that can hide the surprise parts from the guest of honour. No more "who paid the Airbnb?" spreadsheet archaeology.

**Idea 5: PramRoute**
Parents plan routes around kerbs, cobbles, stepped underpasses, narrow shop doors, and lifts that are always out of order — map apps do not know a buggy cannot do stairs. PramRoute crowd-sources stroller-friendly routes with surface, slope, lift, and changing-facility info, and warns when a venue's "accessible" entrance is really a service corridor. Useful for wheelchairs, trolleys, and anyone with a bad ankle too.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: ContextLint 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
A repo now has AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, .cursor/rules, and three other agent instruction files, and they contradict each other — one says pnpm, another npm; one says always run tests, another says never. ContextLint parses every agent/context file, flags contradictory or duplicate directives, dead file paths, and missing commands, and runs in CI so agent instructions do not rot. It also shows which rules are actually loaded by which tool.

**Idea 7: WorktreeWrangler 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Coding agents spawn git worktrees faster than humans can track them, leaving dozens of branches, uncommitted changes, stale node_modules, and mystery ports on disk. WorktreeWrangler gives a local dashboard of every worktree: which agent or session created it, last activity, dirty files, disk usage, and assigned port, with one-click archive/prune and safe cleanup suggestions. It also catches worktrees whose parent branch was merged or deleted.

**Idea 8: SquatWatch 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Typosquatters and brand impersonators publish malicious packages with names one character off from yours, and maintainers usually find out from a victim. SquatWatch watches npm, PyPI, RubyGems, and Go for new packages that mimic your names or README, scores the risk, and gives you a ready-to-send takedown report. It also monitors lookalike domains used in install instructions.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: Cue 📱 MOBILE APP**
Drag performers build numbers by ear — cutting a lip-sync track, marking every reveal and punchline, and hoping they remember which wig goes with which cue. Cue lets them drop in a track, tap cue points on the waveform, attach choreography and costume notes, and run the number in rehearsal mode with haptic countdowns. Niche: drag artists, cabaret and lip-sync performers; competition is basically zero because existing tools are desktop audio editors and notes apps. Funny/viral: drag is built for sharing — a reveal timeline screenshot or rehearsal fail clip is native TikTok content.

**Idea 10: MorrisBook 📱 MOBILE APP**
Morris sides carry their repertoire in photocopied tune sheets, WhatsApp threads, and the memory of one person who knows the figures. MorrisBook gives each side a shared dance library with figures, calls, tune recordings at practice speed, kit checklists, and gig RSVPs. Niche: morris, border, rapper, and sword sides — small, fiercely loyal, and currently served only by PDFs and static databases. Funny/viral: morris is a perennial "what is happening" meme, and a polished app would get shared for the novelty before people realise it is genuinely useful.
