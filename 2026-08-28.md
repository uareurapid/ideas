## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: DonationReceipt**
Millions of people drop off bags of clothes and housewares at thrift stores every year but never claim the charitable deduction, because itemizing each item's fair-market value is a tedious spreadsheet slog. DonationReceipt lets you snap photos of the pile before you donate, auto-IDs items and estimates IRS-compliant fair-market values, and generates a ready-to-file receipt (including Form 8283 for big donations) at tax time. The "you donated $800 and claimed $0" problem, solved in the parking lot.

**Idea 2: ClosureCatch**
On snow days and storm mornings, parents frantically check three websites, a robocall, and the local news to learn whether school is delayed, closed, or running an early dismissal — and the answer differs by district and school type. ClosureCatch aggregates closures, 2-hour delays, and early-dismissal notices across every district in a county and pushes a single notification the moment yours changes, with optional childcare-backup reminders. Hyperlocal, unglamorous, and exactly what every parent checks at 6 AM with one eye open.

**Idea 3: PlaydatePal**
Parents of young kids coordinate playdates through a mess of texts that never resolves — whose house, what time, any allergies, who's authorized to pick up, and "I'll bring snacks but mine won't eat gluten." PlaydatePal lets two (or three) families propose overlapping free windows, share a one-tap emergency/allergy card per child, confirm pickup authorizations, and rotate hosting duties — without turning a simple playdate into a scheduling summit. Distinct from carpooling and childcare: it's the social logistics of "can our kids hang out Saturday."

**Idea 4: EstateSalePricer**
Families settling an estate or running a garage sale have no clue whether grandma's 1960s Pyrex is worth $5 or $500 — and either get fleeced by a liquidator or throw out valuables. EstateSalePricer is an instant reference for used goods: snap a photo of glassware, furniture, tools, or collectibles and get a realistic resale price range (thrift-store vs. collector value) so you price with confidence. The pricing knowledge that used to live in the heads of a few antique dealers, now on your phone.

**Idea 5: RentalGuard**
Rental car companies regularly charge customers for scratches and dings that were already there — and unless you documented the car's condition at pickup, you can't fight it. RentalGuard walks you through a 60-second timestamped, GPS-stamped photo/video walkaround at pickup and drop-off, storing a tamper-evident record you can produce to dispute any bogus damage fee. The "they charged me $400 for a scratch I didn't make" defense, built before you ever leave the lot.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: PerfDiff 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Performance regressions ship silently — a code change that adds 300ms to your API or 2MB to your bundle goes unnoticed until users complain, and then you're bisecting commits by hand. PerfDiff runs as part of CI on every release: it benchmarks endpoints, page loads, and bundle size against the previous build, flags any regression with the exact commit that caused it, and blocks the merge if a threshold is breached. Continuous integration for speed — so you find the slowdown in a PR, not in a support ticket.

**Idea 7: BranchJanitor 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Every active repo accumulates a graveyard of branches — merged-but-never-deleted, abandoned PRs, and feature branches that drifted weeks behind main and now carry half-finished work nobody remembers. BranchJanitor scans your Git host weekly and produces a cleanup report: which branches are safe to delete (fully merged), which PRs are stale, and which unmerged work is silently diverging from main and becoming a merge nightmare. The Git hygiene tool that prevents the "whose branch is this and can I delete it?" conversation.

**Idea 8: InfluencerKit 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Indie devs know micro-creators can drive their first real users, but finding the right niche YouTubers, TikTokers, and newsletter writers — then tracking outreach, review copies, and license codes — is a chaotic spreadsheet job. InfluencerKit searches creators by niche and audience size, tracks your outreach pipeline (emailed → interested → posted), and manages review-copy/license-code distribution with unique redemption links so you know exactly which creator drove signups. Creator marketing for founders who can't afford an agency but need to stop doing cold outreach into the void.

**Idea 9: HeatRank 📱 MOBILE APP**
The chili-head community — the people who collect 50+ hot sauces and chase ever-higher Scoville levels — has no app for the one thing they're obsessed with: their own heat tolerance. HeatRank is a hot sauce collection tracker with Scoville units and tasting notes, plus a personal "heat tolerance chart" that graphs your progression from sriracha to Carolina Reaper over months of increasingly unwise decisions. **Niche:** a passionate, social, extremely online subculture with zero dedicated software. **Viral angle:** "My app says I survived 1.4M Scoville in March and now Tabasco tastes like ketchup" is the exact flex that gets screenshotted across every spicy-food group, and the "rate how much this hurt" pain-meter is built for challenge videos.

**Idea 10: MagnetFish 📱 MOBILE APP**
Magnet fishing — hurling a strong magnet into canals and rivers to haul up bikes, safes, and the occasional WWII relic — is a booming hobby coordinated entirely through scattered YouTube channels and Facebook groups, with no purpose-built tool. MagnetFish lets you log hauls with photos and GPS, map productive pull spots (shared as vague "zones" so rivals don't poach your stretch), and track a running "treasure value" of everything you've fished out. **Niche:** a fast-growing, intensely social subculture with essentially zero modern apps. **Viral angle:** "Today I pulled a gun safe, three bikes, and an unexploded ordnance out of the canal" is inherently the kind of content that makes outsiders go "wait, that's a real hobby?" — and every shocking haul is a free ad for the app.
