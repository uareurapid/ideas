**Idea 1: BarberPassport**
When you move cities or your barber retires, you literally cannot describe the haircut you've had for years — "short on the sides" gets you a completely different head. BarberPassport is a personal haircut history: snap photos of your fresh cut from every angle after each visit, note the clipper guard numbers, fade style, and products used, so any new barber anywhere in the world can replicate it exactly. The one file that survives the death of your barber-client relationship.

**Idea 2: BeautyRota**
Root touch-ups every 6 weeks, gel nails every 3, brows every 4, lash fills every 2-3, waxing, spray tans — people run their entire beauty routine off a mental calendar that constantly slips, so they show up with grown-out roots to every event. BeautyRota is a recurring maintenance calendar for personal grooming: log each service with its cadence, get reminders when something's due, and keep a per-salon history of exactly what was done (colour formula, nail shape, lash style) plus any allergies or notes. The upkeep calendar that keeps you perpetually "just been."

**Idea 3: HolidayCardList**
Physical Christmas and holiday cards are a dying art being kept alive by people who genuinely love them — but the address list lives in a decade-old spreadsheet, nobody remembers who they sent to last year (so someone gets skipped or double-sent), and tracking who replied is guesswork. HolidayCardList is a dedicated card-sending address book: store mailing addresses with household names (including kids' names and spelling), log sent/received per year, auto-flag "they sent us one but we forgot them," and generate a printable envelope-label batch. The sentimental logistics layer for the last people on Earth still sending real cards.

**Idea 4: TicketAppeal**
Private parking charge notices and traffic fines are designed to be paid without question — the appeal process is buried, the deadlines are tight, and the companies bank on you not knowing that a huge percentage of tickets are successfully overturned on technicalities (unclear signage, grace periods, incorrect details). TicketAppeal photographs the ticket and the sign, instantly assesses contestability against the specific rules, and generates a legally-referenced appeal letter with the right grounds — then tracks the deadline and sends reminders. The €60-100 per ticket that parking companies count on you paying out of confusion.

**Idea 5: LibraryDue**

A tiny utility for the surprisingly large, quietly passionate community of people who still actually go to the library.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: CertKeeper 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Every mobile developer has been burned by the Apple signing nightmare: a provisioning profile expired overnight, a distribution certificate was revoked by a teammate, or a device UDID isn't registered so TestFlight builds fail for one tester. CertKeeper is a signing asset manager for iOS/Android teams: track all certificates and provisioning profiles with expiry alerts (30/7/1 day), see which app ID and devices each profile covers, and get a "this is about to break your CI build" warning before it does — instead of discovering it at 11pm the night before a release. The one class of mobile-deploy failure that's 100% predictable yet somehow always a surprise.

**Idea 7: BuildRadar 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
CI build times creep up silently — a cache key that stopped matching, a test suite that grew 3x, a Docker layer that rebuilds every run — and most teams only notice when the feedback loop is already 20 minutes and developers are alt-tabbing away mid-build. BuildRadar profiles every CI step across time: shows per-step duration trends, flags steps where cache hits suddenly dropped, identifies the slowest and most-flaky steps, and suggests concrete fixes (cache the right paths, parallelize these tests, split this Docker layer). Continuous performance review for your pipeline, so "why are builds so slow" stops being a question with no data behind it.

**Idea 8: QueryDoctor 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Slow database queries hide in production logs long before they become incidents, and most teams only find them when a page starts timing out under load. QueryDoctor connects to your slow-query log (Postgres/MySQL), groups queries by fingerprint, ranks them by cumulative cost, and — for each heavy query — inspects the schema and execution plan to recommend missing indexes, rewrite strategies, or a N+1 pattern it detected. Like a DBA on call that reads your logs every week and hands you a prioritized "fix these three queries" list, without needing to hire a full-time database expert.
