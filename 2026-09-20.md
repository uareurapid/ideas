## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: PiercingCare**
Tattoo aftercare apps exist, but nobody serves the massive body-piercing crowd. PiercingCare tracks healing milestones, cleaning schedules, jewelry downsizing dates, and lets users photo-log irritation or keloid bumps to show their piercer. A gentle "you're touching it again" nudge turns a real infection-risk habit into a retention loop.

**Idea 2: SlabTracker**
Trading-card, coin, and comic collectors pour money into PSA/BGS/CGC grading but track submissions in spreadsheets or notes. SlabTracker logs each card's condition, submission status, turnaround time, and current market comps, and doubles as an insurance-ready inventory list. A money-adjacent hobby with obsessive users and no focused tool.

**Idea 3: NotaryNear**
Finding a notary on short notice is a scramble — banks are hit-or-miss, mobile notaries hide behind opaque pricing. NotaryNear shows nearby notaries and commissioners of oaths with transparent per-signature pricing, hours, and same-day booking. Simple, high-intent demand whenever someone buys a house, signs a POA, or notarizes travel docs.

**Idea 4: BracesLog**
Orthodontic patients (and their parents) get vague verbal instructions about rubber-band wear and wire pokes. BracesLog tracks elastic wear time, logs poking-wire pain and wax usage, and counts down to adjustment appointments with milestone photos. A two-year, high-engagement treatment window with no dedicated app.

**Idea 5: MoleMap**
Melanoma outcomes hinge on catching changes early, yet most people never systematically track their spots. MoleMap lets users photograph moles/skin marks on a body map and re-photograph them monthly, flagging size, color, or border changes to discuss with a dermatologist. A genuinely useful health tool with recurring, low-effort engagement.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: EvalLoop 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
As devs ship LLM and agent features, "does it still work?" becomes a vibes check. EvalLoop is a CI-native evaluation harness: golden datasets, scored regression runs on every prompt change, and drift alerts when outputs silently degrade. Think unit tests for your AI features, wired into the same pipeline as your code.

**Idea 7: LoadSpike 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Indie devs find out their app dies under load on launch day or Black Friday, not before. LoadSpike is a cheap soak/load-testing service that replays realistic traffic spikes against a staging URL and reports where it breaks. Catches the classic "works on my laptop, melts at 5k concurrent" failure before it's public.

**Idea 8: SponsorMatch 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Indie founders know niche newsletters and podcasts convert, but finding them and estimating overlap is manual guesswork. SponsorMatch indexes small newsletters, podcasts, and YouTube channels by audience, then estimates overlap with your ICP and pulls typical sponsorship rates. Turns "sponsor a newsletter" from a hunch into a measurable channel.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: BubbleButler 📱 MOBILE APP**
Aquarium and aquascaping hobbyists test water daily and log everything in notebooks, yet have no focused app. BubbleButler logs ammonia/nitrite/nitrate/pH, predicts water-change days, tracks livestock, and — for the comedy hook — generates in-app fish obituaries when a tankmate passes. Small but deeply passionate niche (freshwater, reef, planted tanks) with no dominant incumbent, and the funeral feature is eminently shareable.

**Idea 10: RemoteDetective 📱 MOBILE APP**
Every household has a drawer of mystery remotes and no idea which one turns on the TV. RemoteDetective identifies a remote (or a pile of them) from a photo, names the device it belongs to, pulls up the exact manual, and explains what the baffling buttons do. Zero competition, universally relatable, and "rate my remote drawer" is built-in viral content.
