## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: ReportWit**
Teachers spend 20+ hours a term writing report card comments that all end up sounding identical. ReportWit takes a teacher's rough notes per pupil plus the curriculum objectives, then produces varied, specific comments in the school's tone — and flags phrases reused across the class so no two reports read the same. Sells to individual teachers at £6/term, then to schools as a whole-staff licence.

**Idea 2: ClubSafeguard**
Volunteer-run sports clubs get shut down by paperwork: DBS checks, first-aid certificates, coaching badges, and safeguarding training all expire on different dates and nobody tracks them. ClubSafeguard is a dead-simple register that texts the volunteer and the club secretary 60/30/7 days before expiry, with a printable evidence pack for the league or council. Every grassroots club in the country has this problem and solves it with a spreadsheet that one person maintains badly.

**Idea 3: FixQueue**
Repair Cafés run on paper intake forms: what's broken, who owns it, which volunteer took it, what part is needed, whether it was fixed or binned. FixQueue replaces the clipboard with a photo-based intake queue, parts-needed board, and a follow-up text when the part arrives — plus a "repair stories" export that helps the café win grant funding. Thousands of these run monthly worldwide and essentially all use paper.

**Idea 4: GroomNotes**
Dog groomers keep critical client knowledge in their heads: which dogs bite for nails, which coats mat if cut short, which owner always complains. GroomNotes is a per-dog record with before/after photos, handling notes, coat history, and an automatic "last groomed X weeks ago" nudge — plus a shareable card the owner receives. Existing pet software is built for vet clinics and boarding kennels, not one-chair groomers.

**Idea 5: TempTale**
Small cafés, delis, and community kitchens are legally required to keep fridge and freezer temperature logs, and most still use a clipboard that gets filled in retrospectively. TempTale lets staff snap a probe reading twice a day, timestamped and tamper-evident, auto-flags out-of-range readings with a corrective-action prompt, and exports a due-diligence PDF for the environmental health officer. Cheap, boring, and mandatory — the best kind of recurring revenue.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: SlopScore 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Teams are shipping AI-generated code faster than anyone can review it, and the result is "works but unmaintainable": duplicated helpers, hallucinated APIs behind try/catch, missing error paths, and copy-pasted business logic. SlopScore audits PRs and repos for these AI-code smells, produces a per-author/per-directory score, and opens fix PRs for the mechanical wins. It's the missing QA layer for the vibe-coding era, and it sells to engineering managers who can feel the debt but can't measure it.

**Idea 7: DocProof 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Every API doc has code samples that quietly stopped working two releases ago, and nobody notices until a customer complains. DocProof runs every example in your docs against a sandbox environment in CI, diffs the actual response against the documented one, and fails the build with a precise "this snippet on page X is stale" report. It turns documentation from a liability into something with tests.

**Idea 8: CodemodKit 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Every company that ships an SDK eventually faces a breaking change and hand-writes a migration guide that nobody reads, while customers stay on the old version for years. CodemodKit ingests two versions of your API surface, generates real codemods (jscodeshift/ast-grep) plus a migration guide and a "this repo needs upgrading" checker customers can run themselves. It shortens upgrade cycles for the vendor and removes a genuinely hated task from their DevRel team.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: Conkered 📱 MOBILE APP**
Conker fighting — drilling, stringing, and smashing horse chestnuts — has an actual world championship and a fanatically seasonal UK subculture, and zero serious software. Conkered is a conker registry and tournament bracket: photograph your conker, log its hardening method (vinegar, oven, "secret"), record every battle, and build a lineage chart of which conker beat which on the way to champion. Niche: a few thousand obsessive players for six weeks a year. Funny/viral: the "conker autopsy" and family-tree-of-a-nut visuals are extremely shareable, and the hardcore-vs-casual gap makes for great content.

**Idea 10: ChangeRing 📱 MOBILE APP**
Church bell ringing has ~40,000 active ringers, a dense method-learning curve, and almost no mobile software — learners practice with rope-and-doll simulators on desktop or not at all. ChangeRing is a pocket method simulator with haptic "bell" timing, a tower finder with practice-night times, a striking-error score, and a quarter-peal log that auto-fills the ringers' association records. Niche: tiny, ageing, and desperate for new young ringers. Funny/viral: "learn to ring church bells on your phone" is an irresistible demo clip, and the leaderboard of towers rung is a natural bragging mechanic.
