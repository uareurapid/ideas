## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: NameNest**
Naming things (babies, pets, brands, D&D characters) is a group-chat nightmare of endless suggestions and zero decisions. NameNest turns it into a bracket tournament — friends vote head-to-head on name matchups until a winner emerges. No more "I'm fine with whatever" followed by "no not that one."

**Idea 2: QuietBooth**
Remote workers constantly need quiet spots for calls while traveling or between meetings. QuietBooth maps verified low-noise locations — library study rooms, hotel lobbies with good acoustics, coffee shop back corners with outlets — rated by noise level, privacy, and WiFi quality. Think "Google Maps for not sounding like you're in a wind tunnel."

**Idea 3: SecretMenu**
Every restaurant has off-menu items and ordering hacks that only regulars know. SecretMenu crowdsources these hidden gems — the buttered-popcorn Frappuccino, the In-N-Out animal-style fries, the local pho spot's secret spicy broth. Community-verified submissions with location tags so you know what to ask for before you walk in.

**Idea 4: AtticAtlas**
You packed everything into labeled boxes two years ago. Now you need the Christmas lights and can't remember which of 15 identical bins they're in. AtticAtlas lets you snap photos of each box's contents when storing, tag items, and print QR code stickers. Months later, scan the box or search "Christmas lights" instead of opening every bin in a dusty attic.

**Idea 5: AllergyPassport**
Traveling with food allergies means awkwardly pointing at Google Translate while a confused waiter squints at your phone. AllergyPassport generates a clean visual card in 30+ languages showing your allergies with universal icons. Show it to waitstaff, print it, or screenshot it. Designed with input from allergy communities — no more "does this have nuts?" charades in countries where you don't speak the language.
