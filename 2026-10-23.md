## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: ThankYouLedger**
Wedding and shower gifts arrive with cards that get separated from the presents, so couples end up guessing who gave what. ThankYouLedger scans gift and card photos into one guest-gift list, tracks which thank-you notes are sent, and exports addressed labels. Most people use a spreadsheet today; almost nobody has built a clean, etiquette-aware app for it.

**Idea 2: SignSnap**
Parking signs stack arrows, time windows, permit exceptions, and street-cleaning rules into one confusing pole, and a wrong read costs a $75 ticket. Point the camera at the sign and it parses the rules against your location and the current time, answering "yes, until 4pm" in plain English. It also saves a timestamped photo as appeal evidence — SpotAngels maps spots, but doesn't decode sign semantics.

**Idea 3: KitOrder**
Volunteer-run youth teams and clubs collect sizes, names, jersey numbers, and payments across group chat, then someone rebuilds it all in a spreadsheet. One link lets parents pick sizes, add names and numbers, pay, and see the final roster; the club exports a printer-ready order. Existing team stores are tied to big uniform vendors, leaving small clubs underserved.

**Idea 4: RenewalRadar**
Families discover expired passports, visas, sports physicals, and camp forms the week before travel or tryouts. Upload a photo of each document; it extracts the expiry date, warns at 90/30/7 days, and gives a country- or state-specific renewal checklist. Boring, universal, and currently handled by guilt and calendar reminders.

**Idea 5: GuestPass**
Apartment buildings and neighborhoods require visitor parking registration, and guests get towed because nobody remembers the portal or the deadline. Hosts create a pass with plate and time window, send a link, and get a reminder before it expires. Property managers have clunky back-office portals; residents have nothing.

**Idea 6: AgentAudit 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Teams now merge code written by Cursor, Claude Code, and Copilot agents with no record of which agent, prompt, or human review touched it. **Idea 7: ManifestMint 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Apple keeps expanding privacy manifest and SDK signature requirements, and indie iOS developers find out at submission time. Drop in an Xcode project; it detects required-reason APIs and third-party SDKs, generates PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy files, and flags missing signatures before archive. Narrow, deadline-driven, and poorly served by generic compliance tools.

**Idea 8: VersusKit 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Developer tools win searches like "X vs Y", but hand-building comparison pages is tedious and they go stale within weeks. Give it two products; it pulls public feature and pricing pages, drafts an honest comparison table with schema markup, and monitors competitors for changes that require an update. Competitor-monitoring tools watch; VersusKit produces the actual page.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: PaintShame 📱 MOBILE APP**
Miniature painters mix custom paint recipes — brand, ratio, layers — then can't recreate them months later, while the backlog of unopened boxes quietly grows. Log recipes per miniature with photos, track paint inventory, and get a shareable "paint shame" score for unpainted models. Niche: Warhammer and D&D painters; existing apps are inventory-first, and the backlog-guilt angle gives it a funny identity that spreads in hobby groups.

**Idea 10: DuckLog 📱 MOBILE APP**
Jeep owners "duck" each other by leaving rubber ducks on windshields, but there's no way to know where a duck came from or how far it traveled. Tag or scan a duck, log the drop location, and follow its journey when the next owner checks in — plus leaderboards for most-ducked models and a live sighting map. Niche: the Jeep community has millions of owners and active groups but no dedicated ducking app; the "duck passport" mechanic is inherently funny and viral.
