## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: CrecheQueue**
Parents put their names down for nurseries and childminders while still pregnant, then spend two years phoning for updates and comparing contradictory wait-time estimates. CrecheQueue logs every application, nudges the provider on a sensible schedule, and shows families nearby wait-list lengths, fees, and sibling-priority rules in one place. It also gives nurseries a shared wait-list view instead of a notebook and a stressed manager.

**Idea 2: FibreCheck**
Anyone renovating an older home can drill into asbestos without knowing it, and the guidance online is a mess of scary PDFs and conflicting advice. FibreCheck asks about the building's age and the materials being disturbed, reviews photos of suspicious panels, insulation, or floor tiles, and returns a clear risk rating with next steps and local licensed surveyors. It turns a blind panic into a ten-minute check before the sledgehammer comes out.

**Idea 3: SnagHero**
New-build homeowners have a short warranty window to report crooked walls, leaking drains, and doors that do not close, but most snag lists live in angry emails and never get fixed. SnagHero guides a room-by-room inspection, captures dated photos and measurements, produces a professional snagging report, and tracks each defect until the developer signs it off. It also gives buyers a paper trail for the warranty claim they will inevitably need.

**Idea 4: DoseRight**
Children's medicine is a minefield of different strengths, weight-based doses, and the "when did I last give Calpol?" question at 3 AM. DoseRight stores each child's weight, calculates the correct dose for common pain and fever medicines, enforces the maximum doses per day, and shares a live handover summary with grandparents, babysitters, or the other parent. It is a simple safety net for the most sleep-deprived people in the house.

**Idea 5: GiftAidGo**
Charity shops lose thousands in Gift Aid because paper declarations are illegible, addresses are incomplete, and volunteers never know which donations qualify. GiftAidGo scans and validates declarations at the counter, links them to donor records, flags ineligible items, and exports an HMRC-ready claim. It replaces a shoebox of forms with a checkout flow a weekend volunteer can actually use.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: PushProof 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Push notifications behave differently in the foreground, background, and when the app is force-quit — and every new iOS or Android release quietly changes the rules. PushProof runs a notification test matrix on real devices: permission states, rich media, deep links, grouping, and delivery when the app is killed, then flags exactly which combination failed. Mobile teams stop discovering broken notifications from support tickets after a campaign sends.

**Idea 7: ReceiptLab 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
In-app purchases and subscriptions fail in the boring edge cases: restored purchases, interrupted transactions, sandbox receipts, refunds, and account switching. ReceiptLab gives mobile developers a test harness that replays those scenarios against StoreKit and Google Play Billing, validates receipt handling, and shows whether entitlements end up in the right state. It catches "paid but still locked out" bugs before users do.

**Idea 8: FontAudit 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Startups pull fonts from Figma, random GitHub repos, and designer folders with no idea whether the licence covers web embedding, mobile apps, or server-side rendering. FontAudit scans the codebase and design assets, identifies every font file and family, matches it to its licence terms, and flags missing attribution, restricted embedding, or unlicensed use. It produces a compliance report before a lawyer or a font foundry sends the letter.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: CoverStory 📱 MOBILE APP**
Niche: manhole-cover collectors and infrastructure spotters — a genuinely global hobby, especially in Japan where decorated manhole covers and collectible manhole cards have a devoted following. CoverStory logs covers with GPS, photos, pattern and maker notes, builds a personal map, and lets collectors trade duplicates and chase rare municipal designs. Low competition because the audience is small, local, and currently organised through niche blogs and paper maps. Funny/viral because "I flew to Osaka for a drain cover" is inherently shareable, and the feed of bizarre municipal art writes its own posts.

**Idea 10: CricketTea 📱 MOBILE APP**
Niche: amateur cricket clubs, where the quality of the tea is a serious competitive matter and away teams judge every ground by its sandwiches, scones, and urn strength. CricketTea lets players rate club teas across categories, photograph the spread, and build a league table of the best teas in the county, with dietary notes and fixture-day recommendations. Low competition because cricket apps focus on scoring and fixtures, while the tea is left to group-chat folklore. Funny/viral because "soggy bottom at away fixtures" reviews and a fiercely argued Tea of the Season award are perfect club-banter content.
