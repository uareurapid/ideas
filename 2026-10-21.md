## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: BorderKit**
Driving through several European countries means juggling vignettes, low-emission-zone stickers, winter-tyre rules, mandatory safety kits, and toll transponders, each with its own website and fines. BorderKit takes your route and vehicle, outputs exactly which permits and stickers you need before each border, links to the official purchase pages, and keeps the receipts in one trip folder for the police stop you hope never happens.

**Idea 2: BulkyBook**
Getting rid of a sofa or a fridge is a maze of council booking forms, kerbside rules, and wildly varying private skip prices, and most people give up and fly-tip. BulkyBook checks your postcode's council rules and next collection slot, then compares licensed man-and-van and skip options with real prices, so the sofa leaves legally and cheaply.

**Idea 3: GrantReady**
Small charities miss funding because grant deadlines, eligibility rules, and required documents live across dozens of funder sites and someone's inbox. GrantReady tracks every funder's cycle, flags which applications you're eligible for, and stores reusable answers and attachments so a two-hour form becomes a twenty-minute one.

**Idea 4: CPDLog**
Nurses, accountants, teachers, and engineers must prove continuing professional development at renewal time, but evidence is scattered across certificates, emails, and memory. CPDLog captures a photo or calendar entry as you go, tags it to the right competency framework, and generates the renewal-ready report with hours, reflections, and missing gaps highlighted.

**Idea 5: DonorNext**
Blood donation eligibility rules are confusing, sessions are scattered, and people only remember they're allowed to donate when a campaign is already over. DonorNext checks your eligibility from a short questionnaire, tracks your next eligible date, finds nearby donation sessions, and pings you when your blood type is short — turning good intentions into a booked appointment.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: RuleSmith 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Every repo now has an AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md, and most of them are stale fiction: dead commands, renamed paths, contradictory instructions that make coding agents wander off. RuleSmith lints instruction files against the actual repo, then runs a small sandboxed task suite to prove whether an agent really follows each rule, and reports which lines are load-bearing versus decorative.

**Idea 7: WhoUsesThis 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Before deleting an endpoint, feature flag, or plan tier, teams guess who depends on it and usually guess wrong. WhoUsesThis joins request logs and product analytics to real accounts, ranks affected customers by MRR and contract size, and drafts the exact deprecation notice and migration checklist — so the scary "can we remove this?" meeting takes ten minutes.

**Idea 8: MeterProof 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Usage-based pricing leaks money quietly: dropped events, duplicate retries, late batches, and customers billed for the wrong unit. MeterProof replays your metering events against each invoice before it's sent, shows the dollar gap per customer with the specific events that caused it, and alerts when a metering pipeline stops reporting entirely.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: NitWatch 📱 MOBILE APP**
Headlice sweep through primary schools every term, but the only tracking system is a panicked class WhatsApp group and a note nobody admits to sending. NitWatch lets parents anonymously report a case by class or year group, shows an outbreak heatmap for their school, and gives a treatment checklist plus a "safe to return" date. Niche: primary-school and nursery parents; low competition because school software is admin-facing and nobody has built the anonymous parent layer that makes reporting tolerable. It is funny and viral because lice are universally disgusting and universally denied — the "outbreak map" and a tongue-in-cheek "Patient Zero" badge spread through class groups on their own.

**Idea 10: GlazeBook 📱 MOBILE APP**
Hobby potters mix glazes from recipes measured in grams, fire them on schedules, and then lose the results in a notebook and a camera roll full of test tiles. GlazeBook stores glaze recipes with material percentages, logs firings and cone/atmosphere, and lines up before/after photos of each test tile so you can actually reproduce the one that didn't run off the pot. Niche: hobby potters and small studio techs; low competition because existing glaze software is desktop-era and priced for production studios, while mobile options are generic notes apps. It is funny and viral because glaze failures are spectacular and endlessly shareable — the "cone 6 disappointment" gallery, kiln-opening reveals, and cursed colour results are built-in social content.
