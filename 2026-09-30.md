## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: PriceBack**
Everyone's felt it: you buy something, and two days later it's $40 cheaper. Most retailers and credit cards offer price-drop protection, but almost nobody files the claim because it's tedious. PriceBack watches your purchases, detects drops within the protection window, and files the refund request for you — you just approve.

**Idea 2: HeirloomKeep**
When your late mother's ring needs resizing or your grandfather's watch needs servicing, finding a trusted jeweler/watchmaker is a guessing game — and your insurance needs documented appraisals. HeirloomKeep stores photos, appraisals, and receipts, tracks repair/servicing schedules, and matches you to vetted local jewelers and watchmakers with transparent pricing.

**Idea 3: SubPlan**
A substitute teacher walking into an unfamiliar classroom has zero context — no seating chart, no allergy list, no "the printer only works if you kick it." SubPlan lets a teacher build a one-page emergency plan (classroom rules, rosters, lesson notes, key contacts) that a sub can open instantly on their phone. Districts and sub agencies would pay for the peace of mind.

**Idea 4: TickCheck**
After a hike or a dog walk, most people do a half-hearted tick check and then spend three days anxiously Googling bullseye rashes. TickCheck logs the date, location, and photo of any bite, then reminds you of the symptom-watch window and stores everything a doctor needs if it turns bad. Turns post-hike paranoia into a clean, shareable record.

**Idea 5: FareWise**
Commuters quietly overpay hundreds a year because they can't tell whether a monthly pass, pay-as-you-go, or a fare cap actually saves money across bus, rail, and ferry. FareWise pulls your actual travel patterns and does the math, then pings you before auto-renewals when a cheaper option exists. Dead-simple, genuinely saves money.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: ShipSign 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Indie desktop and mobile devs lose release days to macOS notarization, signing cert expiry, and provisioning profile hell — and a single expired cert silently kills auto-updates for all your users. ShipSign audits every cert, entitlement, and notarization status across your release channels, warns you 30 days before anything expires, and gives you a one-screen fix checklist.

**Idea 7: TalkTrack 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Speaking at conferences is one of the highest-leverage ways a dev founder builds a brand, but tracking CFP deadlines, acceptance statuses, and travel stipends across dozens of conferences is a spreadsheet nightmare. TalkTrack aggregates open call-for-papers, tracks your submissions and outcomes, and reminds you of deadlines before they close.

**Idea 8: DiffScale 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Every team has hit a 4,000-line "review this" PR that nobody can safely approve, but nobody catches it until it's already opened. DiffScale is a CI status check that scores every PR on size and complexity, flags unreviewable mega-PRs, and suggests split points before the review request goes out.

## Mobile App Ideas (2)

**Idea 9: Antlantis 📱 MOBILE APP**
Ant keepers (a real, obsessive subculture over at r/antkeeping) manage queen health, colony growth, feeding, and humidity with nothing but paper notes and forum posts. Antlantis tracks each colony's census, feeding schedule, and nest conditions, with an "escape drill" alert when the lid is ajar and a queen-spotting photo mode. Tiny audience, zero dedicated apps, and "my colony just hit 2,000 workers" is inherently shareable.

**Idea 10: SweepStreak 📱 MOBILE APP**
Metal detectorists log finds in their head and lose the story of every outing — "today I dug a 1974 penny, a pull tab, and emotional closure." SweepStreak is a GPS-tagged finds journal with streak counters, a "what is this thing?" community ID feature, and shareable dig-day summaries. Underserved niche (r/metaldetecting is huge, but existing apps are generic GPS or land-permission tools), and the find photos are made for viral sharing.
