## General Ideas (5)

**Idea 1: ChargeCheck**
EV drivers still can't trust charging networks — a "working charger" on the map is often broken, blocked, or has a payment terminal that rejects your card. ChargeCheck is a crowd-sourced reliability layer for EV chargers: drivers report out-of-order stalls, broken connectors, and payment failures in one tap, and see a "last confirmed working" timestamp before they detour off their route. The Waze-for-chargers that stops you gambling your remaining range on a charger that's actually dead.

**Idea 2: HemLine**
Finding a good tailor or alterations shop means asking friends or gambling on generic reviews, and nobody knows what hemming trousers or taking in a jacket should actually cost. HemLine is a local tailor directory with real pricing references by alteration type, fitting-appointment booking, and a photo-based "what needs fixing" brief you can send ahead of time. The gap between "this suit doesn't fit" and "here's exactly who fixes it, and what it should cost."

**Idea 3: RecycleRight**
Recycling rules vary wildly by municipality, and most people guess wrong — greasy pizza boxes, yogurt tubs, and "biodegradable" packaging get tossed incorrectly every single day. RecycleRight lets you scan or photograph any item and get your specific area's verdict: rinse and recycle, trash, or special drop-off — plus a plain-English reason why. The "wait, can I even recycle this?" answer, localized to your actual postcode instead of generic national advice.

**Idea 4: WhistleBook**
Youth sports leagues run on a handful of volunteer referees and officials who juggle assignments, game fees, and certification renewals through group texts and spreadsheets. WhistleBook is a scheduling and pay ledger for officials: accept or decline assignments, track game fees and mileage owed, get certification-expiry reminders, and export a season summary for tax time. For the under-appreciated backbone of every youth league that currently lives in a chaotic group chat.

**Idea 5: ChopLog**
Musicians practice constantly but have no structured record of what they worked on, at what tempo, or how a piece is progressing — so lessons and personal goals drift week to week. ChopLog is a practice journal with a built-in metronome log, a repertoire tracker (piece, target tempo, last tempo hit), and weekly "minutes practiced" summaries you can share with a teacher. The practice diary that turns "I noodled for 40 minutes" into measurable, motivating progress.

## Developer Tools (3)

**Idea 6: Statusly 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Indie developers want a professional status page, but Statuspage is enterprise-priced and UptimeRobot is bare-bones — so most just post "we're aware" on Twitter after users have already complained. Statusly combines uptime monitoring, a clean hosted status page on your own domain, and ready-written incident templates ("degraded performance," "scheduled maintenance") you can publish in one click. The trust signal that tells customers "we have our act together" without a $99/mo enterprise contract.

**Idea 7: DeliverabilityKit 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Indie SaaS onboarding emails land in spam because SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are misconfigured — and founders don't find out until signup-to-activation conversion is mysteriously low. DeliverabilityKit checks your sending domain's DNS records, runs a test send through the major inbox providers, scores your deliverability, and walks you through each fix with copy-paste config snippets. The "why do my welcome emails go to spam?" debugger, compressed into a ten-minute checklist.

**Idea 8: AuthLab 🛠️ DEV TOOL**
Every developer has hit "Google login just redirects to a blank page" — OAuth flows fail silently, with a dozen edge cases (expired tokens, scope mismatches, redirect-URI typos) and no clean way to test them locally. AuthLab is a local OAuth sandbox: spin up mock identity providers (Google, GitHub, Slack) with configurable failure modes, walk through your redirect flow step-by-step, and see exactly which callback, state, or scope is breaking. The tool that turns OAuth debugging from "add console.log and guess" into a deterministic test.

**Idea 9: RelativeRoulette 📱 MOBILE APP**
Every family gathering arrives with the same landmines — "still single?", "when are you having kids?", your uncle's latest conspiracy theory — and nobody has a tool for surviving them gracefully. RelativeRoulette generates polite deflection scripts for invasive questions (graded from "gentle redirect" to "full shutdown"), a one-tap fake emergency call that rings your phone with a rehearsed excuse, and a "safe topics" generator for whoever you're stuck next to at dinner. **Low competition:** fake-call apps are generic and deflection-script tools don't exist — nothing is built for the specific awkward-social dynamics of family gatherings. **Funny/viral:** the scripted comebacks are instantly screenshot-able ("I'll have kids when my houseplants stop dying, thanks for asking") and every introvert with a holiday dinner on the calendar is the target market.

**Idea 10: ToastSmith 📱 MOBILE APP**
Writing a best-man or wedding toast is terrifying — you want it funny, heartfelt, and 100% free of lines that will get quoted for the wrong reasons for decades. ToastSmith generates a personalized speech from a few prompts (relationship, vibe, one embarrassing-but-not-too-embarrassing story), with a "roast level" dial and a "cringe guardrail" that flags any line likely to land badly. **Low competition:** generic AI writing tools exist, but a wedding-speech-specific generator with roast/cringe calibration is completely untouched. **Funny/viral:** people will screenshot both the generated roasts and the guardrail's warnings — "this story is 87% likely to get you uninvited from the next family event" is the exact kind of warning that gets shared everywhere.
